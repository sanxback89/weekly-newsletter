Supply Chain – Trade CRITICAL February 9, 2026 USTR

United States and Bangladesh sign reciprocal trade agreement

Summary: The United States and Bangladesh signed a landmark reciprocal trade agreement on February 9, 2026 — the first in South Asia — reducing tariffs from 20% to 19% with provisions for zero-tariff textile imports using U.S.-produced cotton.

Details:

  • Reciprocal tariff reduced from 20% to 19% on originating goods
  • Zero-tariff mechanism for textile/apparel made with U.S. cotton and man-made fiber
  • Original tariff was 37%, reduced to 20% in August 2025, now 19%
  • Bangladesh commits to purchase $3.5B of U.S. agricultural products and $15B of energy products over 15 years
  • Bangladesh to accept U.S. vehicle safety standards, FDA certificates for medical devices
  • Agreement goes into force 60 days after internal formalization
  • If rescinded, tariffs reset to 37%

Supreme Court hears US tariffs case; potential impact on apparel industry

Summary: The U.S. Supreme Court heard arguments on the legality of emergency tariffs, with conservative justices signaling skepticism about presidential authority to impose tariffs under IEEPA.

Details:

  • Average U.S. apparel import tariff reached 26.4% (up from 14.7% pre-2025)
  • Weighted average on apparel/footwear spiked to 54%, eased to 36%
  • China-specific apparel tariff: 69.1% (unprecedented)
  • 76% of fashion executives say tariff response is #1 industry factor for 2026
  • Estimated $35B total tariff duties if import volumes remain unchanged
  • Only 3% of U.S. apparel currently manufactured domestically

Supply Chain – Sustainability HIGH February 2026 PW Krystian

EU bans destruction of unsold clothing and footwear from July 2026

Summary: Under the EU's Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), large companies will be banned from destroying unsold clothing and footwear from July 19, 2026, with Digital Product Passports also required.

Details:

  • Ban applies to large companies from July 19, 2026
  • Medium-sized companies covered from July 19, 2030
  • Micro/small enterprises exempt
  • Digital Product Passport (DPP) required for textiles: material composition, environmental footprint, recyclability info via QR code
  • Textiles-specific delegated act expected to apply late 2028

EU Empowering Consumers Directive bans greenwashing from September 2026

Summary: The EU's Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive (ECGT), banning misleading environmental claims, must be transposed into national law by March 2026, with an effective start date of September 2026.

Details:

  • Bans sustainability labels not based on certification schemes
  • Bans unverified claims about future environmental performance
  • France imposing eco-score labeling from October 2026 (fines of 5% of annual revenue for non-compliance)
  • EU also advancing Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) for textiles — expected final approval early 2026
  • UKFT warns 2026 marks "decisive shift" in sustainability regulation for fashion
